Applied Sevā Standard

100 hours. Supervised. Documented.

The Semester II framework includes a supervised 100-hour service-learning standard. It also requires 17 portfolio submissions, pathway-specific competency evidence, learner reflection, and mentor or supervisor review. This is the approved academic framework, not a claim that all volunteer time automatically earns credit.

Individual placements, schedules, supervision, partner agreements, and funding remain in development until confirmed. Public volunteering is available separately from the apprenticeship.

How It Works

Study → Practice → Direct Experience → Observation → Reflection → Application → Continued Study.

Students first complete the required preparation, agree on a suitable supervised placement, document approved service, and return to reflection and review. Pathway availability depends on active projects and mentors.

Community is our campus · Mentored learning

Learn with people. Grow across places.

An apprentice may begin with responsibility in one setting and develop new abilities in another. School mentors, community educators, site stewards and field experts help connect learning to real practice. Dream Connections is the relationship network that supports those opportunities—not a promise of automatic placement or employment.

01 · Existing strengths

Start where you contribute

Identify leadership experience, interests, access needs and responsibilities already demonstrated.

02 · Supported growth

Learn alongside mentors

Agree on goals, scope, supervision and feedback with educators and community site leads.

03 · Cross-school learning

Connect ecology and wellness

For example, garden stewardship can inform accessible community yoga education and vice versa.

04 · Professional independence

Develop your own practice

Build instructional, ethical, organizational and relationship skills that support sustainable independent work.

Learning example · Ecology and community wellness

A garden operations lead can expand through mentored stewardship at a community garden while developing teaching experience through nearby Yoga for Life classes. The mentor helps connect observation, lesson preparation, reflection, feedback and responsibility across both settings.
