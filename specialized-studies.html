Foundational text · Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

Four states. One inquiry into consciousness.

The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad offers a concise framework for examining waking (jāgrat), dreaming (svapna), deep sleep (suṣupti), and turīya—the fourth, understood as the underlying reality beyond the three ordinary states.

These teachings inform philosophical and contemplative study of dream experience and self-inquiry. They are not presented as modern scientific findings or as a scientific classification of sleep.

Research direction · Developing

Dream cognition, sleep & contemplative practice.

A developing research direction for studying dream experience, cognition, memory, and literature on dream therapies. Contributors may help gather sources, organize questions, document reflections, and build relationships with qualified researchers and practitioners.

Educational inquiry is distinct from providing therapy. Any future therapeutic service or participant research will require qualified leadership, a defined scope, and appropriate review.

Our study also compares what classical sources teach with what contemporary sleep and cognitive science can measure. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad’s account of turīya remains a philosophical and contemplative teaching, not an established sleep stage.
