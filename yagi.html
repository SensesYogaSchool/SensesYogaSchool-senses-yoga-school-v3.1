How it began

One neighbor's idea. Many hands helping it grow.

The Yoga Arts Garden Initiative (YAGI) began when community members wanted to bring a garden initiative to life. Through Senses Yoga School's School of Prakṛti, the invitation grew into an ecological wellness and learning experience grounded in shared care for Garden Park.

Teagan Rowe and Sam Kautzman contributed in-kind operational, educational and culinary knowledge as the initiative took shape. Their early stewardship helped YAGI unfold through practical work, conversation, observation and community learning. We honor those contributions as part of the initiative's history, without implying that historical roles are current assignments.

Our approach is a paramparā of stewardship: knowledge carried forward through participation, mentorship, ecological responsibility and communal sustainability. The community is our campus, and the garden is a living teacher.

Restoration in progress · 2026

What has taken root.

14raised garden beds in the restoration footprint
9beds being evaluated for community stewardship gifting
Mar–Novapproximate outdoor season, guided by weather

Phase 1 restoration has begun. The initiative has brought organic soil from Larsen Landscaping and Topsoil into the 14 garden beds, supporting a renewed foundation for planting and learning. Nine beds are being evaluated for in-kind stewardship opportunities with community organizations, clubs, groups and neighbors. These opportunities are under review, not yet awarded.

YAGI is developing a shared stewardship model that welcomes community participation and aims to preserve a free-picking-for-all approach to the garden's harvest, subject to seasonal growing conditions and responsible care of the beds.

On selected YAGI days, support from Gathering Table and Darius Simmons Garden has helped connect neighborhood produce and bread distribution with garden activity. Contributions and availability vary by day.

Development roadmap · Proposed, not funded

Garden Park's next chapters.

Phase 1 · Active restoration

Restore, protect, teach

Continue bed rehabilitation and condition-based repairs; improve pollinator fencing, educational signs, seasonal growing supports and accessible site care. Toddler swing repairs and structural improvements require qualified review and site approval.

Phase 2 · Future development

Art and year-round growing

Explore a community mural led by Anyssa Camacho, honoring Concordia neighborhood heritage, and a long-term community greenhouse concept. Solar integration, water collection, site design, funding and permissions require further planning.

Phase 3 · Long-term vision

A solar-supported wellness stage

Envision a solar-supported community stage for yoga, music, education and neighborhood gatherings, alongside a rainwater collection system. Engineering, accessibility, maintenance, permits and neighborhood input will guide feasibility.

These phases are a working vision developed with community partners, not approved construction projects. The school is seeking appropriate partner guidance, funding and site permissions before committing to improvements.

A seasonal rhythm

We follow the weather, not only the calendar.

YAGI's outdoor stewardship season generally unfolds from March through November, depending on weather, soil conditions, growing cycles, volunteer capacity and community needs. Spring welcomes preparation and planting; summer brings shared care and learning; autumn offers harvest, restoration and reflection. Winter supports planning, curriculum and future partnerships.

This seasonal rhythm follows the school's 2026–2027 Annual Operations Plan while remaining responsive to the living conditions of the site.

From the meeting table to the garden beds

Our plans are shaped by experience.

YAGI's development is documented through field observations, team meetings and ongoing stewardship records. In June 2026, the team discussed weather resilience, soil-and-compost balance, volunteer coordination, water access, youth learning and practical garden themes. The team shifted from symbolic bed concepts toward functional areas including pollinator habitat, Foodways, wellness and youth discovery.

By September, the leadership team was refining how Garden Operations, Foodways, Arts & Storytelling and Teacher-Steward work could be documented without duplicating effort. These are records of a developing initiative, not a public roster of current responsibilities.
