About Senses · Living History

A school taking root in public life.

Senses Yoga School is a decentralized community school rooted in yoga and expressed through practice, inquiry, ecology, relationship, creativity, and service.

Its story is not contained in one building. It is visible in the places people gather, the practices they return to, the gardens they tend, the art they make, and the relationships that slowly become a school.

Chapter I · Practice
Begin where you are.

Senses Yoga School · living practice

The school began from a simple premise: practice can become education when people are given room to explore, return, reflect, and grow.

Our Mission

Healthy individuals. Healthy communities. Healthy ecosystems.

Our mission is to make yoga and contemplative education part of ordinary community life—not something reserved for a particular body, income, building, belief, or stage of life.

We begin with the individual because attention, breath, movement, reflection, and self-knowledge change how a person meets the world. But the work does not stop at individual wellness. Practice becomes meaningful when it changes how we relate: to another person, to a neighborhood, to work, to food, to land, and to responsibility.

That is why Senses holds wellness, education, ecology, arts, and service inside one school. The inner life and the shared world are not separate subjects.

Community as Campus

Education beyond four walls.

A yoga mat can become a laboratory for self-awareness. A garden can become a classroom in reciprocity. A park can become a place of practice. A library can become a place of inquiry. A neighborhood can become a living school.

“Our Community Is Our Campus” is therefore not a metaphor for having no building. It is an educational commitment: learning should remain close to life. We meet people in places that already carry memory, relationship, work, beauty, difficulty, and possibility.

Decentralization allows the school to grow through relationship before infrastructure. A future sanctuary may give the ecosystem a home, but the campus already exists wherever practice and responsibility are being cared for.

Chapter III · The Vedic Arts
Kalā · The Arts as Practice
Movement · Sound · Story · Image · Craft · Ecology · Contemplation

Senses approaches the Vedic Arts as a living field of human cultivation: practices through which attention, imagination, embodiment, memory, relationship, and meaning can be trained together.

01 · Body & Movement

Embodiment is a way of knowing.

Āsana, breath, gesture, rhythm, walking, strength, stillness, and sensory awareness allow ideas to become lived experience. The body is not merely prepared for philosophy; it participates in philosophy.

At Senses this appears through Yoga For Life, Heroic Breathwork, public practice, adaptive movement, and the physical labor of stewardship.

02 · Sound & Listening

Attention can be tuned.

Mantra, voice, music, silence, rhythm, and deep listening change the quality of attention. Sound becomes both an artistic medium and a contemplative discipline.

This thread continues through MOGA, Nāda study, breath, chanting, listening practices, and the soundscape of community life.

03 · Story & Image

Stories give practice a human shape.

Epic, myth, archetype, photography, drawing, visual storytelling, journaling, and dream inquiry help learners encounter questions that cannot always be reduced to instruction.

Heroic Yogi, the Rāmāyaṇa, archetypal study, Dreamscaping, field photography, and creative documentation keep this dimension alive.

04 · Land, Craft & Service

Creation becomes relationship.

Growing food, tending soil, cooking, making signs, restoring a site, building useful things, documenting change, and caring for shared space turn creativity outward.

Through YAGI and Foodways, ecological stewardship becomes both practical education and an extension of yoga into the living world.

Why the Arts Belong Together

Yoga is larger than a sequence of poses.

Traditional Indian knowledge systems did not always divide human development into the modern categories of fitness, philosophy, art, ecology, spirituality, and education. Practices of movement, sound, story, ritual, observation, craft, contemplation, and community life could inform one another.

Senses does not attempt to recreate another historical world. We ask a contemporary question instead: what happens when these modes of learning are allowed to speak to one another again?

From self-discovery to relationship

A learner may first encounter yoga through the body. Breath introduces the interior world. Philosophy gives language to experience. Story and archetype expand imagination. Art makes reflection visible. Ecology asks the learner to encounter something beyond the self. Service asks whether practice has changed the way responsibility is carried.

Chapter IV · The Founder

Luan Seguim-Arnold

Founder & Executive Director

Luan’s journey began across cultures—born in Brazil and raised in the United States—and was shaped through the arts, community and environmental work, and sustained self-practice.

His path into yoga deepened through study and relationship with teachers connected to Rishikesh, India, and Milwaukee. Rather than separating yoga from ordinary life, his work increasingly asked how contemplative practice could become community education, ecological stewardship, service, and leadership.

Senses Yoga School grew from that question. The founder remains part of the journal, but not its only author: students, stewards, teachers, partners, neighbors, gardens, weather, and community life continue writing the school with him.

Chapter VI · An Evolving Ecosystem

The school is still becoming.

Today Senses lives through community partnerships, public practice, parks, gardens, educational programs, professional settings, creative work, and shared leadership across Milwaukee.

The ecosystem is intentionally developmental. Public practice can become sustained study. Study can become service. Service can become apprenticeship. Apprenticeship can become leadership. A partner can begin by hosting one class and later help build an enduring community learning pathway.

A sanctuary-centered home remains a longer-term direction, but the present school is already alive wherever its practices and relationships are being cared for.
