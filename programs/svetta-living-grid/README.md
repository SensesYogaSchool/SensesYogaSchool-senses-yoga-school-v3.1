# Svetta Living Grid — Community Energy & Conscious Technology Pilot

**Senses Yoga School | School of Ecology (lead) × School of Specialized Studies (technical co-lead)**  
**Stage:** Open call for proposal collaborators and prototype partners · **Status:** Concept / feasibility research, not an operating energy service  
**Initial planning target:** One sponsored community node designed to explore serving up to 20 households.

## Invitation to collaborate

Senses Yoga School invites community members, educators, students, renewable-energy professionals, licensed electricians, engineers, environmental stewards, technologists, neighborhood organizations, and prospective sponsors to co-develop a community-centered proposal and a safe, measurable prototype for **Svetta Living Grid**.

The vision is solarpunk: technology made with people and for people, supporting ecological regeneration, human well-being, and dignified access to essential resources. Svetta is not a judge of households or beliefs. It helps people understand evidence, uncertainties, options, and the needs of one another without moral scoring, surveillance, or coercion.

## Two-school educational collaboration

**School of Ecology — program lead:** renewable-energy literacy, water collection and stewardship, garden/farm integration, community participation, ecological lifecycle analysis, and local field research.

**School of Specialized Studies — technical co-lead:** conscious technology education, offline-first Android app, privacy-preserving local communications, energy-monitoring interfaces, hardware integration, accessible documentation, and prototype evaluation.

The pilot is a joint educational initiative rather than a new standalone school. Steward appointments and partner commitments remain to be confirmed.

## Proposed system

1. **Svetta Family app:** Offline-first family knowledge and a clear, nonjudgmental view of measured household energy and water-system data. Local control, informed consent, and no sale of personal data.
2. **Home machine:** A proposed solar-and-battery monitoring/control interface, with possible integration of rainwater/fog collection pumps or sensors where appropriate. Physical equipment and capabilities require design validation.
3. **Community node:** A sponsored neighborhood renewable-energy installation concept with a **20-home planning target**. A wireless communication network can carry information; household electrical power requires a separate, professionally engineered distribution system.
4. **Voluntary expansion connectors:** Households may eventually opt to fund approved communications or electrical extensions. Connectors do not create energy or guarantee more capacity; expansion must be assessed for safety and sufficiency.

## Sponsorship and access principles

The aspiration is a **one-time sponsored capital installation** with no required household subscription or per-use charge for participating families. This is a proposed funding model, not a verified zero-lifetime-cost claim. Maintenance, insurance, battery replacement, repairs, seasonal shortfalls, regulatory obligations, and equitable access must be accounted for in the feasibility study. Sponsorship must not confer ownership of household data or preferential rights over neighbors.

## Phase 0 — Community proposal and feasibility

Seeking collaborators to:
- Define household energy service levels, including essential loads and seasonal needs.
- Assess local solar resource, winter production, storage sizing, and a credible 20-home load profile.
- Identify safe electrical architecture, utility/interconnection requirements, site ownership, and relevant codes.
- Model upfront costs, lifecycle maintenance, replacement reserves, and sponsorship governance.
- Explore rain/fog collection suitability, water quality, and actual pump/treatment loads.
- Define accessible community participation and safeguards against exclusion.
- Select prototype scope and measurable educational outcomes.

**Deliverable:** A community-reviewed feasibility proposal with assumptions, risks, costs, roles, and a go/no-go decision for physical installation.

## Phase 1 — Educational prototype

Build an Android demonstration with simulated energy flows and an optional **read-only** interface to approved solar/battery monitoring equipment. Test local connectivity, privacy controls, offline use, and understandable, nonjudgmental recommendations. Demonstrate water-system monitoring where feasible. Do not connect homes electrically or enable autonomous power switching in this phase.

**Deliverable:** Reproducible prototype, safety and privacy review, educational materials, and community feedback.

## Safety, truthfulness and governance

- Do not describe the 20-home node, free energy, or household range as operational or guaranteed.
- Electricity cannot be delivered to neighboring homes over ordinary Wi-Fi; physical power sharing requires engineered electrical infrastructure and relevant approvals.
- Preserve essential household energy, health, water, and emergency needs.
- Require qualified professionals for electrical design, installation, protection, and interconnection.
- Use actual field photographs only with permission and accurate captions; do not present illustrative images as documented installations.
- Report measured energy use and limitations transparently. Avoid assigning moral worth or ecological virtue scores to people.
- Protect children and families: local-first data, minimal collection, meaningful consent, and no sponsor access to private household records.
